The brand system
behind the site

Every colour, letterform and component on this concept traces back to a decision, and most of those decisions trace back to something the practice already owns. This page shows the working.

See it in use The process

01 · The mark

Genuine vector, and what we did with it

The practice supplied its logo as two SVGs: a horizontal lockup and the disc mark on its own. Both turned out to be real vector geometry, which is rarer than it should be.

Primary lockup, light ground

Dark ground — a required variant, not a nicety

What was already right

Real paths, no live text, no embedded rasters. Nothing had to be redrawn, so the mark on this site is the practice's own geometry rather than an approximation of it.

What needed fixing

The exporter flattened each gradient into about 202 separate stops, and wrote every coordinate to six decimal places. That was 89 KB for a two-colour ramp.

The result

Two stops instead of 202, coordinates rounded to a precision finer than the screen can show: 89 KB down to 48 KB, and the disc mark from 62 KB to 16 KB. Pixel-identical.

The supplied wordmark is a hardcoded #004AAD, and the gradient’s deep end is #2E3192. Both sit close enough to the navy used across this site that the logo would half-disappear on it. That is why a dark-ground variant exists at all — it is a legibility fix, not a style option.

02 · Colour

Read off the logo, not picked to taste

The first pass at this concept had only a raster logo to work from and sampled it by peak chroma. When the vector arrived, the real values could be read directly — and the sampling had been close, but not exact.

  • Process Cyan#00AEEF Gradient start. The brand’s loudest asset — used as a fill, never as small text.
  • Deep Indigo#2E3192 Gradient end. Sampling had guessed #213F9A.
  • Wordmark Azure#004AAD The letterform fill. Sampling had guessed #0253A5.
  • Cyan Ink#0072A8 The working partner. See the contrast note below.
  • Signal Gold#E5AC2B From the lockup’s ring. Reserved for outcomes — what you get.
  • Ink Navy#0E2140 Deepest surface. Carries the dark bands and the dark cards.
  • Bone#FAF6EF The page ground. Warm and faintly skeletal — a long way from clinical white.

The blue–gold rule

The palette carries the site’s organising idea. Blue is the clinical side — measurement, the scan, the science. Gold is the human side — plain English, the outcome, you.

It is applied literally. On the services page every card runs the same three lines: the practice’s own clinical name in blue, a plain-English translation, then what you actually get, in gold.

Process Cyan cannot carry small text. #00AEEF on white measures about 2.5:1, well under the 4.5:1 the WCAG AA floor requires. It is gorgeous as a fill and unusable as a label. Every place the brand blue has to carry actual words, it is #0072A8 instead — and on dark grounds, #7FE0FF.

03 · Type

Two faces, one job each

The first version of this site paired Fraunces with Inter. It read as dry — correct, but with nothing to say. The replacement gives the display voice some actual character and keeps the body text quietly excellent.

Bricolage Grotesque

Aa

Strong bones. A body that keeps up with you.

Display. Variable on optical size and width — opsz is tracked to the rendered size so the big cuts tighten instead of looking like blown-up text.

Plus Jakarta Sans

Aa

A ten-minute scan tells you years earlier, while there is still plenty you can do about it.

Body. Open apertures and a tall x-height, set at an 18px base rather than the usual 16 — this practice’s patients skew over fifty, so legibility is a functional requirement, not a preference.

  • H2 · clamp(2–3.15rem)Everything on offer

One component was deliberately left empty. The reference layout this design borrows from ships a star rating, a review count and a completed-works counter. InsureSPR publishes none of those, so the same components carry facts that are actually true — how long the scan takes, what it does not involve, when the practice is open. Inventing social proof for a real medical practice is not a design decision, it is a false claim.

05 · Process

How it was made

Five passes, in order. Each one produced something the next one needed.

  1. 01

    Read everything the practice publishes

    Homepage, about, blog, contact and booking flow. Every service name, every claim and every contact detail was pulled verbatim first, so that the design had real content to be shaped around rather than placeholder text.

  2. 02

    Find the one idea

    The practice writes in clinical language for an audience deciding whether a scan is worth booking. That gap is the brief. Everything else follows from it: keep every clinical name exactly as written, and pair each with what it means and what you get.

  3. 03

    Build the system before the pages

    Colour read off the logo, a type scale, then a component kit — actions, cards, pills, stat blocks. Pages assembled from the kit stay consistent for free; pages designed one at a time drift.

  4. 04

    Write it, then cut it

    Every factual claim was checked back against the practice’s own material, and anything that could not be traced was removed rather than softened. That is why this concept shows no pricing: the real site publishes none.

  5. 05

    Measure, do not eyeball

    Seven widths from 320px up, measured by script rather than judged from a screenshot. An earlier site in this series passed a desktop-and-mobile check while overflowing by 46px at 320 — which is exactly the kind of thing a screenshot will not tell you.

06 · Straight answers

What is real and what is ours

The practice’s own

The name, mark and tagline. Every service name. The address, hours, phone number and email. The three programmes. The blog article titles. Claims like “one consultation at one price” and “BMI is population-based and outdated” are the practice’s own words, paraphrased.

Ours

All layout, colour application, typography, iconography and motion. Every plain-English translation and outcome line. The photography, which is licence-free stock chosen to show people at the ages the practice actually serves. And this page.

InsureSPR Health is a real practice. This concept is not their official site, is not affiliated with them, and nothing on it is medical advice. The booking and contact forms have no backend and say so on the page rather than quietly swallowing a real person’s health enquiry.
