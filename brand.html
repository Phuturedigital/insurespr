The system behind the site

Every colour and letterform here traces to a decision, and most of those trace back to something the practice already owns. This page shows the working.

See it in use

01 · The mark

Genuine vector, and what we did with it

The practice supplied real vector artwork — rarer than it should be, and it meant nothing had to be redrawn. But the exporter had flattened each gradient into about 202 separate stops and written every coordinate to six decimal places.

Verified first

The ramp was linear

Before collapsing 202 stops to two, the middle stop was checked against a straight blend of the endpoints: rgb(21,113,193) against rgb(23,112,193). Inside 2/255, so two stops reproduce it.

Then rounded

Finer than the screen

Coordinates cut to two decimals. On a 300-unit viewBox drawn at 185px the error ceiling is 0.003% — well under one pixel, so invisible.

Result

89 KB down to 48 KB

And the disc mark from 62 KB to 16 KB, pixel-identical. A dark-ground variant exists because the wordmark is a hardcoded #004AAD that vanishes on the navy — a legibility fix, not a style option.

02 · Colour

Read off the logo, not picked to taste

An earlier pass had only a raster to work from and sampled it by peak chroma. When the vector arrived the real values could be read directly — and the sampling had been close, but not exact.

  • Process Cyan #00AEEF Gradient start. The brand’s loudest asset — used as a fill, never as small text.
  • Deep Indigo #2E3192 Gradient end. Sampling had guessed #213F9A.
  • Wordmark Azure #004AAD The letterform fill. Sampling had guessed #0253A5.
  • Cyan Ink #0072A8 The working partner. See the contrast note below.
  • Ink Navy #0A1C3D Deepest surface — the dark panels and the footer.
  • Ground #EEF3F9 The page itself. Pale blue-grey, so white cards read as objects on a surface rather than as the page.
Process Cyan cannot carry small text. #00AEEF on white measures about 2.5:1, well under the 4.5:1 the WCAG AA floor requires. It is gorgeous as a fill and unusable as a label — so wherever the brand blue has to carry actual words it is #0072A8 instead, and on dark grounds #7FE0FF.

03 · Type

Three cuts, one job each

Display

Outfit

Set light and large. That combination is most of the register — the same sizes set bold would read as a much louder brand than a diagnostic practice wants.

Body

IBM Plex Sans

A genuine healthcare and scientific face. Open apertures and a tall x-height, set at a 17px base rather than the usual 16 — these patients skew over fifty.

Measurement

IBM Plex Mono

Every label, duration and figure on the site. Mono because measurement is the subject — it reads as a clinical readout, which is exactly what “Precision Healthcare” claims.

04 · The line we did not cross

The reference was a pricing page. This one is not.

The layout this concept follows sells four tiers, at $450, $2,800, $6,500 a year and $120 a month. InsureSPR publishes no prices at all — its own booking system has no services configured — so the programme cards keep the structure and drop the number.

The same rule removed the star rating, the review count and the completed-works counter that layouts like this ship with. Inventing any of those for a real medical practice is a false commercial claim, not a design placeholder.

05 · Process

How it was made

01

Read everything the practice publishes

Homepage, about, blog, contact and booking flow — pulled verbatim first, so the design was shaped around real content rather than placeholder text.

02

Find the one idea

The practice writes clinically for an audience deciding whether a scan is worth booking. That gap is the brief: keep every clinical name, add what it means and what you get.

03

Build the system before the pages

Colour read off the logo, a type scale, then a component kit. Pages assembled from a kit stay consistent for free; pages designed one at a time drift.

04

Cut anything that cannot be traced

Every factual claim checked back against the practice’s own material, and anything unsourced removed rather than softened.

05

Measure, do not eyeball

Widths from 320px up, checked by script. The real test is whether the document scrolls sideways — not whether some element extends past the viewport, because a horizontal scroller’s children legitimately do.

06

No face under text

An audit script finds every place text paints over a photograph. Page heads put copy and picture in separate columns, so a headline across someone’s eyes is structurally impossible rather than something to keep re-checking.
