Health programmes

Three ways in, depending on where you are

These are the practice’s own three programmes, in its own words — plus what each one actually involves.

InsureStrong

Build and maintain healthy bone and muscle mass

Who it’s for

Anyone who wants to know their starting point and build on it — including people who train and want real numbers rather than a bathroom scale.

What’s included
  • DXA bone density scan
  • DXA body composition — muscle, fat and bone, limb by limb
  • Fitness and nutrition guidance built on your own measurements
  • A repeat scan later, so you can see whether it worked

Ask about pricing. The practice does not publish its fees, so this concept does not invent any.

InsurePrevent

Stop bone and muscle loss before it starts

Who it’s for

People who want their fracture risk assessed properly — posture, mobility and bone strength together — before a stumble becomes a break.

What’s included
  • Nurse-led osteoporosis clinic consultation
  • Posture, flexibility and balance assessment
  • Bone density scanning
  • Ongoing management of primary and secondary osteoporosis

One consultation, one price. The practice runs this as a single appointment with the scans and assessments included — its own stated approach.

InsureReclaim

Restore what has been lost, so you can live boldly

Who it’s for

People rebuilding after a fall, a fracture or a long stretch of doing less — where the goal is getting specific things back.

What’s included
  • A personal plan built around goals you set
  • Monthly measurable checkpoints

  • Health coaching between appointments

How your plan gets built

Four steps, about an hour in the practice, and you leave with the result explained rather than posted to you a week later.

Step 1

We talk first

What you have noticed, what you are worried about, what you take. Five minutes, and it decides what is worth measuring.

Step 2

You lie down

Ten minutes on an open table, fully clothed. A scanning arm passes above you. No tunnel, no needles, no dye.

Step 3

We read it together

Your numbers, on screen, explained while you are still in the room — not a report you decode alone at home.

Step 4

You leave with a plan

What to do, what to change, and when to come back and check. Written down, in the same plain language.

Said plainly

You should not need a medical degree to understand your own body

The practice’s services keep their proper clinical names here. Each one gains a sentence explaining what it actually is, and a line on what you get out of it.

DXA bone density

A ten-minute scan measuring how strong your bones are, so thinning is caught while there is still time to act on it.

You leave knowing whether to act now or re-check in a few years — instead of finding out from a fracture.

DXA body composition

The same scan, showing how much muscle, fat and bone you carry and where. What you are made of, not just what you weigh.

A measurement that moves when the work is working. A scale can sit still for months while your body changes underneath it.

Fall-risk assessment

A proper test of how likely you are to fall — posture, flexibility and balance — and which specific thing changes that answer.

A straight answer on your risk and the one or two things that shift it, rather than being told to “be careful”.

Every service, translated

Why this exists

A fall is not the problem. What a fall costs you is.

The practice puts it plainly: many people never regain their independence after a fall and a fracture. Bone loss has no symptoms until something breaks, which is why it gets called the silent disease.

Measuring it is the only way to know early — and early is when there is still plenty you can do.
